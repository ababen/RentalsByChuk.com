Most Realtors treat rentals as a throwaway lead for future sales. **RentalsByChuk does the opposite.** A rentals-only specialist for exactly three cities — West Palm Beach, Palm Beach Gardens, and Jupiter — with deep neighborhood expertise, seasonal strategy, and HOA compliance that no generalist can match.

## 2. Area Research: The Three Cities

### 2.1 West Palm Beach (WPB) — The Urban Core
- **Population:** 126,944 (2025) — added 9,529 since 2020, largest city in PBC
- **Median Age:** 40 (youngest of the three)
- **Median Household Income:** $60,487
- **Renter Households:** 28,479 — **49.8% renters** (vs 30% national)
- **Foreign-born:** 27.2% | Hispanic: 24.6% — bilingual market
- **Character:** Employment hub (finance, legal, healthcare, government), downtown revitalization (Clematis, CityPlace, Brightline station), urban renewal corridors (Northwood, Flamingo Park/El Cid). Demand driven by *jobs*, not just lifestyle/migration. Year-round tenant base independent of snowbird cycle.
- **Key Neighborhoods:** Downtown Clematis Corridor ($2,500-$4,000+), Flamingo Park/El Cid ($2,200-$3,500), Northwood ($1,800-$2,800, revitalization upside), SoSo, Grandview Heights.
- **Growth Driver:** In-migration from higher-cost markets + downtown Class A office + Brightline connectivity.

### 2.2 Palm Beach Gardens (PBG) — The Affluent Golf Enclave
- **Population:** 64,547 (2025) — **+9% / +5,365 since 2020**, fastest grower in PBC. Has now surpassed Jupiter.
- **Median Age:** 51
- **Median Household Income:** $105,635 (highest of the three)
- **Renter Households:** 7,993 (23.4% of 27,475 households); 31,400 housing units, 13.1% vacant (seasonal/second homes)
- **Character:** Master-planned, golf-centric (PGA National, Mirasol, Old Palm, BallenIsles, Frenchman's Creek/Reserve). Families + second-home owners. Very HOA-governed.
- **Key Neighborhoods:** Mirasol, PGA National, Old Palm Golf Club, Frenchman's Reserve, BallenIsles, Central Gardens, Evergrene.
- **Growth Driver:** Perceived safety, schools, golf lifestyle, "better value than Palm Beach island."

### 2.3 Jupiter — The Coastal Premium
- **Population:** ~61,200 (2025) — essentially flat (+0.3% since 2020)
- **Median Age:** 50
- **Median Household Income:** $101,829
- **Renter Households:** 7,004 (23% of 27,991 households)
- **Character:** Coastal, limited land east of I-95 (structural supply constraint), Intracoastal waterfront premium, walkable Abacoa, gated communities (Rialto, Egret Landing, The Bluffs, Admirals Cove). Strongest seasonal rental economy in northern PBC.
- **Key Neighborhoods:** Abacoa (young professionals, walkable), Rialto/Egret Landing/The Bluffs (gated, affluent), Intracoastal/Atlantic waterfront (luxury/estate), Jupiter Farms (rural, equestrian spillover).
- **Growth Driver:** Lifestyle migration from Northeast/Midwest, "soft landing" renters evaluating area before buying, limited new construction.

### 3.2 Market Momentum

- **WPB rent growth:** +2.2% YoY (Apartment List, March 2026) — *outperforming* Tampa/Orlando/Jacksonville which declined. WPB Central: **+8.3%** YoY, asking rents +10% YoY (MIAMI Realtors).
- **PBG median rent:** $2,944/mo (+3.3% MoM, -1.87% YoY). Inventory **-20.29% YoY** — only 687 rentals citywide and falling.
- **Jupiter:** Avg rents 39% above national average. Vacancy ~5%. Premium gated/SFH: 14-28 days to lease if priced correctly. Median days on market for rentals: **61 days** (Oct 2026, PBP) — longest of the four cities tracked, reflecting luxury/estate properties dragging the median.
- **Occupancy:** 94-96% in premium northern submarkets (Jupiter, PBG, N. Palm Beach) — Yardi/MIAMI Realtors March 2026.
- **County median (2BR condo):** ~$2,150 flat YoY. Vacancy ~5.8% (vs 9.1% Miami metro Q2 2026 Census).
- **Houses vs Apartments (WPB, Oct 2026):** Houses avg $3,500 (81% above national avg), Apartments $2,150 — houses recovering to May 2025 peak of $4,000.

### 3.3 Five Structural Trends Defining 2026

#### Trend 1: The Accidental Landlord Tsunami
- Redfin (Feb 2026): **55.9% of WPB for-sale listings sat 60+ days** without going under contract (well above national avg).
- PBC median days on market: **52 days** (April 2026) even as sales volume climbed — divergence = sale-to-rental conversion signal.
- Zillow (March 2026): **2.3% of all US rental listings were previously for-sale** — 3-year high; 7 of 10 highest metros in TX/FL.
- Atlis Property Management (PBG, 600+ doors): Rising first-time landlord inquiries on detached homes **>$750,000** in Jupiter/PBG. Two-tier landlord market forming:
  - *Pandemic buyers (2020-22, ~3% mortgages):* Renting by **choice** — can break even, keep rate, wait.
  - *Post-2023 buyers (7%+ mortgages):* Renting by **necessity** — can't sell without loss, forced to rent.

#### Trend 2: Two Parallel Rental Economies (Annual + Seasonal)
- Every city runs **annual (12-mo, unfurnished)** + **seasonal (Dec-Apr, furnished, 2-3x premium)** markets simultaneously.
- Seasonal: $4,000-$10,000+/mo for winter (Snowbird) vs. annual at lower monthly but year-round occupancy.
- Townhome/small SFH: $2,400-$3,400 annual. Waterfront/estate SFH: $15k-$25k+ annual; seasonal multiples higher.
- Owners must model both — decision depends on furnishing costs, HOA seasonal caps, summer vacancy tolerance.

#### Trend 3: The May/June "Goldilocks Window"
- Snowbirds depart May → seasonal units pivot to annual → **temporary inventory spike late May/early June**.
- Described as "Goldilocks time" to sign a lease before late-summer school-year rush.
- Luxury buildings in Downtown WPB now offering concessions (waived fees, 1 month free) — first summer in years where renters have negotiating power.

#### Trend 4: HOA Complexity Is the Hidden Moat
- PBG/Jupiter are heavily HOA-governed. Typical restrictions: max rentals/year, 6-month minimum, board approval (5-30 days), fees, outright bans.
- Florida Statute §720.306(1)(h) (effective July 1, 2021): Post-2021 rental amendments bind **only** consenting/new owners — *except* 6-month minimum + 3x/year cap which bind **all** owners retroactively. Massively misunderstood.
- Short-term rentals: Combined **12% tax** (6% state sales + 6% PBC tourist tax) + DBPR registration + city ordinances (Jupiter/Juno Beach strict).
- Condo boards cap application fees at **$150/applicant** (§718.112(2)(k)); HOAs have no cap.
- Most landlords list *before* checking HOA — risk fines, injunctions, forced tenant removal.

#### Trend 5: Supply Drying Up, Demand Structurally Intact
- PBC does **not** have Miami's luxury condo oversupply → rents firmer.
- Net absorption (2,383 units) significantly outpaced completions (745 units) — MIAMI Realtors/Yardi March 2026.
- Multifamily completions forecast to **decline** through 2026.
- Jupiter has limited greenfield east of I-95 → structural rent protection for established neighborhoods.
- Northward migration: Renters moving up from Broward/Miami-Dade for "lifestyle value" (less traffic, more space).

## 4. Who Rents Here? Personas

### A. The Relocating Professional (30% of demand)
- From NY/NJ/Midwest, high-income, corporate relocation or self-directed move.
- Rents 12-24 months as "soft landing" while evaluating area/house-hunting.
- Wants: Abacoa, Downtown WPB, gated PBG. Move-in ready, pet-friendly, good schools.
- Tendency: Long tenancy, qualified, financially stable → renews if managed well → **future buyer.**

### B. The Seasonal Snowbird (25%)
- Occupies Dec-Apr only. From Northeast/Midwest, affluent.
- Wants: Furnished, Intracoastal/golf/waterfront, turnkey, concierge.
- Pays: $4k-$10k+/mo standard; $15k-$25k+ waterfront. Peak booking Feb/March, 55-62 days ahead.
- Often becomes annual buyer or repeat seasonal tenant.

### C. The Priced-Out Local Family (25%)
- Can't buy at $700k+ / 7% rates → stuck in rental pool.
- Wants: 3BR/2BA SFH $3,500-$5,000 in Jupiter/PBG (e.g., Abacoa, Egret Landing, Mirasol). Schools matter (Jupiter High, Dwyer, Suncoast).
- Competition: "Fierce" in this band. Long tenancy.

### D. The Accidental Landlord (20% — GOLDMINE LEAD SOURCE)
- Owns $750k-$1.2M home, tried to sell, failed, now forced to rent.
- Profile: No landlord experience, terrified of tenants/HOA/taxes/pricing. Often overprices 10% → 38-52 days vacant → loses ~$2,450/cycle before adjusting.
- Needs: Hand-holding, compliance check, pricing, tenant screening, property prep.

### E. The Downtown Young Professional (WPB-specific)
- Works finance/legal/healthcare/Brightline corridor. Age 25-38.
- Wants: Clematis, Flamingo Park, Northwood. $1,800-$3,500. Walkability, amenities.
- Opportunity: First summer in years with negotiating power (concessions in new luxury buildings).
